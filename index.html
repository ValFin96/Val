What is actually wrong

You do not need another list of marketing ideas.

You need to know what is actually getting in the way.

Maybe you are:

sending traffic to your website without knowing why people are not taking the next step;
posting consistently without seeing how your content moves people towards an offer;
using email, social media and paid advertising, but each channel feels disconnected;
hearing different recommendations from your agency, freelancer or team;
unsure whether the real problem is your message, website, proof, offer or traffic; or
preparing to invest more without knowing what deserves fixing first.

Each part of your marketing can look reasonable on its own. The problem often appears in the connection between them.

  • A social post makes one promise.
  • The website continues a different conversation.
  • The customer still has unanswered questions.
  • Important proof appears too late.
  • The next step is unclear.

So when results are disappointing, the answer often becomes more content, more traffic, a new website or another specialist.

But adding more activity will not fix a customer path that is already losing momentum.

The focus

See what your customer is experiencing from first touch to conversion.

The audit focuses on one priority customer path connected to one core offer and one conversion goal. For example:

  • Instagram content → website → service page → enquiry
  • Paid ad → landing page → product page → purchase
  • Google search → website → proof → consultation booking
  • Content → email → offer page → purchase

The exact path depends on how your business currently attracts and converts customers.

The purpose is not to audit every marketing channel you own. It is to understand whether the touchpoints that matter are working together to help the customer progress.

The method

The 3D Method.

Decode

First, I build a working picture of the customer and the decision they are trying to make. I review the evidence you already have to understand:

  • who this path is designed for;
  • what the customer is trying to achieve;
  • what problem or priority brings them into the journey;
  • what they need to understand about your offer;
  • what they need to believe before they trust you;
  • the questions or objections that may slow them down; and
  • the action you ultimately want them to take.

This may include customer reviews, FAQs, sales insights, existing feedback, marketing materials and available business data.

Where something is supported by evidence, I will say so. Where something is still an assumption, I will make that visible too.

Diagnose

Next, I map the selected customer path across your marketing and look for the places where momentum may be weakening. Depending on your business, I may review your website, landing pages, social content, email, paid advertising, product or service pages, enquiry or booking flows, and checkout or conversion steps.

I look for issues such as:

  • inconsistent messages between touchpoints;
  • unclear positioning or value propositions;
  • missing or poorly placed proof;
  • unanswered customer questions;
  • weak calls to action;
  • confusing handoffs between channels;
  • unnecessary conversion friction;
  • content that attracts attention without progressing intent;
  • gaps between discovery and conversion; and
  • obvious departures from relevant conversion and marketing best practices.
Direct

Finally, I turn the diagnosis into a practical 30-day action plan.

You will know what needs attention, what should happen first, who should own it and how to measure whether the change helped.

The action plan

Your 30-Day Customer Journey Action Plan.

This is where the diagnosis becomes something your team can use. For each recommended action, you will see:

What needs to change
The specific page, message, handoff, proof element, campaign step or conversion issue that needs attention.
Why it matters
What the issue may be doing to the customer experience and why it deserves attention.
What to do
A practical recommendation your team, freelancer or agency can implement.
What happens first
Recommendations are sequenced so you do not spend time fixing something that depends on another issue being addressed first.
Who should own it
For example, the founder, copywriter, designer, developer, media buyer, email specialist or agency.
Level of effort
You will see whether the recommendation is likely to be a quick adjustment, a moderate task or a larger piece of work.
Evidence behind the recommendation
I distinguish between findings supported by customer evidence, business data, direct observation, established best practice or a hypothesis that still needs validation.
How to measure progress
Each relevant action includes a success measure so you know what to watch after the change is made.

The sequence

A clear plan for the next 30 days.

Your recommendations are organised into a practical sequence.

Days 1 to 10

Fix the foundations

Address the issues most likely to weaken everything that follows. This could include unclear messaging, broken page hierarchy, missing proof, weak calls to action or an important disconnect between channels.

Days 11 to 20

Strengthen the customer path

Improve the key messages, handoffs and decision points customers encounter as they move towards conversion.

Days 21 to 30

Test and measure

Implement focused tests, monitor relevant behaviour and decide what deserves further investment.

The process

How it works.

The independence

Why look across the whole path?

  • A paid media specialist naturally looks at advertising.
  • A website specialist looks at the website.
  • An email specialist looks at email.
  • A content specialist looks at content.

Your customer does not experience those channels separately. They experience one business.

The 3D Marketing Clarity Audit looks at how those activities connect, where the customer experience becomes weaker and what needs attention before more money is committed.

I am not using the audit to sell you advertising management, a website rebuild, content creation or ongoing implementation. The recommendation may be to invest more. It may also be to fix something you already have first.

The fit

Is the audit right for you?

Designed for established consumer, ecommerce and service businesses that

  • have a proven offer and real customers;
  • use several marketing touchpoints;
  • have enough existing activity and evidence to diagnose;
  • are preparing to improve or invest further in marketing;
  • have the ability to implement recommendations; and
  • want independent direction before deciding what to fund next.

Not designed for businesses that

  • are still pre-launch;
  • need complete marketing management;
  • want a full annual strategy; or
  • require original market research.
